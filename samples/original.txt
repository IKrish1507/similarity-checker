Urban beekeeping has grown quickly over the last decade as more people look for ways to support pollinators in cities. A single hive on a rooftop can hold tens of thousands of bees during the warm months, and those bees will forage across several kilometres of gardens, parks and street trees. Beekeepers in dense suburbs often find that their honey tastes different from season to season because the flowers in bloom change throughout the year.

Keeping bees is not without challenges. Hives need regular inspections to check for pests, disease and signs that the colony is preparing to swarm. Neighbours may worry about stings, so many councils ask beekeepers to register their hives and to keep them a reasonable distance from property boundaries. Despite these rules, interest continues to rise, and community groups now run weekend workshops for beginners who want to start their first colony.
