Learning to bake bread at home takes patience more than skill. The first loaves are often dense or flat, usually because the dough was not left long enough to rise in a warm place. Measuring flour by weight instead of by cup makes results far more consistent from one bake to the next.

A simple loaf needs only flour, water, salt and yeast. Kneading develops the gluten that traps gas from the yeast, and a hot oven with a tray of water underneath helps the crust turn crisp and golden. Once the basics feel familiar, many home bakers move on to sourdough, which uses a living starter instead of packet yeast.
